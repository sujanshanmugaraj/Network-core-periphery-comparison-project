8 / 12 · Degree-preserving double-edge swaps, 10 × M swaps each

Is the group-level gap just a consequence of the degree sequence? We rewire each network 100 times keeping every node's degree. In the rewired copies the gap disappears: k-cores become fully cohesive, fragmentation goes to 1 and brokers vanish, exactly as Łuczak's 1991 theorem says for random graphs. For GrQc, Facebook, Yeast and the power grid the real values lie above all 100 copies, the strongest result a 100-sample test can give. A side finding: the Borgatti–Everett fit is lower in the real networks than in their random versions — so it is the cohesion structure, not the density-based core–periphery fit, that carries non-random signal.
