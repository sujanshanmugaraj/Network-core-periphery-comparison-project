This toy example is the whole paper in miniature. Both measures give every node the value 2, so node rankings agree. But the k-core sees one group while cohesion sees two overlapping groups whose overlap, node X, is a broker. The karate club on the right shows the same thing on real data: coreness and cohesion level coincide at every node, yet the cohesion hierarchy singles out one broker. Proposition 3 in the paper: two k-components share fewer than k nodes, so brokers are always small glue sets.
