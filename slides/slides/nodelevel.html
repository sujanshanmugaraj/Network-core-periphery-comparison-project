First result. The inequality cohesion ≤ coreness always holds, and in real networks it is almost always tight. k-coreness and cohesion level rank nodes practically identically, so if all you need is a node ranking, the cheap k-core is an excellent proxy. Borgatti–Everett is a different animal: its binary labels correlate only weakly with cohesion. Rombach sits in between, and is weakest on the power grid. Our hypothesis H1 said agreement would be lower in spatial networks; that holds for the CP models but not for the k-core.
