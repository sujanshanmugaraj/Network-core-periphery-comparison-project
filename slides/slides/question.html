Two literatures define the core differently

Core–periphery · k-core

The core is where ties are dense

Borgatti–Everett (2000), Rombach et al. (2014), k-core peeling (Seidman 1983). Cheap, ubiquitous, degree-based.

Structural cohesion

The core is what is hardest to pull apart

Moody & White (2003): k-components, maximal groups that survive the removal of any k−1 nodes. Exact, but expensive.

RQ1 Do they agree? · RQ2 Which finds the critical nodes? · RQ3 Does a hybrid help?

2 / 12

The two traditions rarely cite each other. Core–periphery models reward density: many ties, especially among core nodes. Cohesion asks a different question: how many nodes must I remove to disconnect this group? Every k-component lies inside the k-core, but not the other way round. Our three research questions: how much do they agree, which one identifies nodes that matter for keeping the network connected, and does combining them help.
